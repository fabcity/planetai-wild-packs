# Surf v1.1 and pack buttons: design

Status: design, 3 Nov 2026. Not built. Follows `2026-11-02-surf-pack-design.md` (v1, PR #15, running on node #1 since
3 Nov) and core PR fabcity/planetai-node#370 (`{act}`).

## 1. Why

Tomas has three kids and a lot of work and has not surfed for a while. The hard part is choosing: is it worth going,
where, and when. The node should help him decide a day ahead, for tomorrow and the day after, and it gets better from
his answers after a session. Later (not here) it should learn each spot from data only his node collects, to get a
precision the big forecasts miss.

After seeing the first real message (alert #1212, 3 Nov) he asked for four things:

1. say what every number is;
2. recommend time windows that suit him: an intermediate surfer, out of shape;
3. feedback by buttons, and buttons as a node feature any pack can use for its own feedback;
4. feedback on the dashboard too.

Agreed order (3 Nov): this document covers surf v1.1 (part A) and pack buttons in core (part B), built together. A
forecast section on the dashboard for every reading, surf included, is a separate design, next. Learning from local
data comes after a few weeks of answers.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals
- One evening message covering tomorrow and the day after, every number labelled, a best window per spot inside his
  hours.
- Answering after a session takes one tap, on Telegram or on Now, and the journal reads it.
- Buttons are a core contract any pack's rule can use, not something special to surf.

Non-goals
- The forecast section (next design). Local-data correction of the model (later). Push at other hours.
- Changing ρ: surf stays a `warn` rule.

Anti-goals
- **Hiding a good day because it is big.** Tomas, 3 Nov: "you can inform about the size of the waves, do not hide it
  from me, if it is a good condition". Comfort is a label, never a filter.
- A message he has to decode.
- Buttons that record something the node never offered (a forged or stale tap writes nothing).

## 3. Part A: surf v1.1 (pack, `packs/surf/`, wild PR #15)

### A1. Spots

serangan, green-bowl, nyang-nyang.

- Bingin stays in `spots.yml` and is not watched ("no bingin unless asked"). Medewi, Keramas and Sanur stay in the
  file and are not watched.
- New rows, first guesses (my call; the journal corrects them). Positions from OpenStreetMap (Nominatim, 3 Nov), set
  on the reef a little off the named beach point:
  - `impossibles`, Impossibles, at [-8.8085, 115.1075], swell [190, 250], offshore [60, 150], kw [6, 40],
    tide [mid, high], aliases [impossible]. Reef between Padang Padang and Bingin.
  - `nyang-nyang`, Nyang Nyang, at [-8.8400, 115.0945], swell [170, 250], offshore [0, 110], kw [3, 25],
    tide [mid, high], aliases [nyang]. South-facing, more open to swell than the west side of the Bukit.

### A2. Two days

The message covers tomorrow and the day after (local dates, from the same 3-day fetch). Each day is assessed exactly
as v1 assesses tomorrow. Each sensor keeps one set of readings per day: v1's metrics stay for tomorrow, and the day
after gets the same metrics with a `_d2` suffix (`surf_good_d2`, `surf_kw_d2`, …). The meta holds the lines for both
days (`line_en`, `line_en_d2`, and so on). The rule picks up to three good spots per day.

### A3. His hours, the best window, comfort

New pack settings, all in `pack.yaml` `env:` with these defaults (node #1 uses them as they are):

- `SURF_HOURS=08:30-12:00,16:00-18:30`, the hours he can usually get away. An hour `h` (the hourly row at `h:00`)
  is inside a range when `start <= h:00` and `h:00 + 1 h <= end`, so 08:30–12:00 gives 09, 10 and 11, and
  16:00–18:30 gives 16 and 17.
- `SURF_SESSION_H=2`, the length of a session.
- `SURF_COMFORT_KW=15`, about 1.5 m at 13 s or 1.8 m at 10 s. Over it, the line says "big for you right now". It
  never removes a line.

Good hours (my call, builder check): every hour between 05 and 18 that passes v1's gates, not only v1's longest run,
so a spot good 05–08 and 16–17 shows both ("good 05–08, 16–17"). A run still needs two hours.

The best window: among the good hours, the run of `SURF_SESSION_H` consecutive hours inside `SURF_HOURS` with the
lowest mean wind speed; ties go to the earlier. The line's numbers (waves, power, wind, tide) are those of the best
window: means over its hours, the tide of its first hour. Without a window inside his hours, they are those of the
first good run. `SURF_COMFORT_KW` is compared with the same power the line prints. When no such run exists but the spot is good
for two hours or more outside his hours, the line still appears and says when ("good 05–08, outside your hours").
Tomas: "I can try to make time if the surf is good".

Order within a day: lines with a window inside his hours first, then those outside. Within each group, power from
highest to lowest. At most three per day.

### A4. The message

Every number named. English below; Spanish and Indonesian follow the same layout, written the same way v1 writes them.

```
🌊 Surf in the next two days

Wednesday 4 Nov
• Uluwatu, best 09:00–11:00 (good 05–18)
  waves 1.0 m every 13 s, from the SSW
  power 8 kW/m · wind 9 km/h from the E, offshore · tide high
  https://www.windy.com/-8.815/115.087?swell1,-8.815,115.087,12
• Nyang Nyang, best 16:00–18:00 · big for you right now
  waves 1.8 m every 13 s, from the SW
  power 21 kW/m · wind 4 km/h, glassy · tide mid
  https://www.windy.com/…

Thursday 5 Nov
• Balangan, good 05–08, outside your hours
  …

A model, not a surf report. Check before you go.
How was it? Tap below, or write: /act 1212 <your words>
```

- "mixed with 0.6 m every 9 s from the S" goes on the waves line when v1's mixed flag is set.
- A day with nothing good says "Thursday 5 Nov: nothing looks good". The message is sent when at least one of the two
  days has a good spot (v1 sent nothing on an empty day; keep that for both days empty).
- The buttons (A5) answer tomorrow only: the day after comes back as tomorrow in the next evening's message. When
  tomorrow has nothing good the message goes without buttons (my call); `/act` still takes words.

### A5. The surf buttons

Under the message, one row per spot named for tomorrow, then one last row:

```
[ Uluwatu 👍 ] [ 👌 ] [ 👎 ]
[ Nyang Nyang 👍 ] [ 👌 ] [ 👎 ]
[ Didn't go ] [ Surfed somewhere else ]
```

- One answer per spot (my call, builder check): a tap lifts only that spot's row, so a morning at Uluwatu and an
  evening at Nyang Nyang can both be rated. "Didn't go" lifts the whole keyboard. Two people in the house may each
  answer, from their own chat or on Now.
- 👍 good, 👌 ok, 👎 poor. Values `<spot-id>:good|ok|poor`, stage `acted`. `didnt-go` is stage `acknowledged`.
  `elsewhere` is stage `acted`.
- A rating or "Surfed somewhere else" asks one optional line afterwards: "Anything to add? Size, crowd, how you felt.
  One line, or ignore me." For "elsewhere": "Where, and how was it? One line, or ignore me." The reply becomes that
  answer's note.
- Labels are localized (en, es, id) the way the message is.

### A6. Asking about any spot

A new agent script `ask` (`planetai run surf ask bingin`, and listed in `agent_scripts`, so the bot may run it when
asked "how's Bingin tomorrow?"). It fetches now and prints that spot's two days in the message's own format,
whether or not the spot is watched. It stores nothing and does not touch the stamp file, so it never delays the
adapter's next fetch. An unknown name prints the spot list.

### A7. The journal

`review` reads the button answers as well as `/act` notes: `<spot-id>:<rating>` gives the spot and the rating, the
note carries any words. "Didn't go" counts the call as answered and lists no spot, so `review` reads `acknowledged`
answers too. It takes each spot's best window (or good hours) for tomorrow from the alert's sensor meta at answer
time where it can, else from the text, and reads only tomorrow's block of the text, since a spot can appear on both
days. GET `/actions` returns `choice` (B2). The rest of v1's journal (that
morning's numbers by spot, the unanswered list) stays.

## 4. Part B: pack buttons in core (planetai-node, a new PR)

### B1. Declaring them

A rule's SQL may return a column `buttons`: JSON text, an array of rows, each row an array of buttons:

```json
[[{"text": {"en": "Uluwatu 👍", "es": "Uluwatu 👍"}, "value": "uluwatu:good", "stage": "acted",
   "ask": {"en": "Anything to add? …", "es": "¿Algo más? …"}}]]
```

- `text`: a string, or a map by locale. The node picks the alert's locale as it picks the message, falling back to
  `en`.
- `value`: `[a-z0-9:_-]`, at most 40 characters (Telegram allows 64 bytes of callback data, and `al:<id>:` takes the
  rest).
- `stage`: `acted` or `acknowledged`, default `acted`.
- `ask`: optional. A follow-up question whose one-line reply becomes the answer's note.
- At most 8 rows of at most 4 buttons. An invalid `buttons` value is logged and the alert goes without buttons. The
  message is never lost over its buttons.

`tools/check_rules.py` accepts `buttons` as a column no message placeholder names. A pack's tests can validate their
own JSON against the same checker.

### B2. Storing them

- `alerts.buttons JSONB` (nullable), the keyboard as declared, stored with the alert row.
- `actions.choice TEXT` (nullable), the `value` of the button pressed. GET `/actions` returns it.
- Both added in `init.sql` with `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS`, the way the schema already grows.

### B3. Telegram

- When `TELEGRAM_BUTTONS=1` (the bot has announced itself, as for events), a rule alert with buttons goes with an
  inline keyboard. Callback data is `al:<alert_id>:<value>`.
- The bot parses it next to `ev:` and posts `{"alert_id", "choice", "actor"}` to `/actions`. The node looks up
  the alert's stored buttons, refuses a value not among them (400) and a tap on an alert older than 72 hours (409,
  "this question has closed"), takes the stage from the button, and writes one `actions` row with `choice`. It does
  not refuse a second answer: the same choice from the same actor is written once, anything else is a new row
  (my calls, builder check; every pack inherits them).
- The keyboard after a tap: the pressed button's row is lifted, unless that button's `stage` is `acknowledged`, which
  lifts all (surf's "Didn't go"). A pack that wants one answer per alert puts its buttons in one row.
- After a tap: the chat gets "Uluwatu 👍 · Tomas" (the button's text and who pressed), and if
  the button has `ask`, the question is sent and the next text from that chat becomes the note. The bot already does
  this for an event's "Doesn't fit", and this uses the same mechanism.
- Without the bot (`TELEGRAM_BUTTONS` unset), the message goes bare, as now, and `{act}` still gives the typed way.

### B4. Now

- Now lists rule alerts from the last 36 hours that have buttons and no answer yet, under What to do: each alert's text
  and its buttons, drawn from `alerts.buttons`. A press posts the same `/actions` body the bot posts, with the
  household's name from `planetai_actor` as for events. A button with `ask` opens a one-line field after the press.
- Each answer shows under it, "answered: Uluwatu 👍 by Tomas at 12:40" plus the note if there is one. Rows go the
  way they go on Telegram, and the alert leaves the list when no row is left or after 72 hours.
- Same tokens and refusals as the event buttons: from this machine with no token, from elsewhere with ACT_TOKEN or
  ADMIN_TOKEN.

### B5. Docs and visibility

Standing rule (24 Oct): a new feature has a place in the UI, a docs page, and the deployed site. So:

- `docs/site/bot.md`: pack buttons, with one picture.
- CHANGELOG, and the site rebuilt after release.
